Jean-Paul Sartre and the Youth of May 68

A philosopher who refused every easy label, a man who stood on barricades and declined the Nobel Prize. Jean-Paul Sartre and the Youth of May 68 revisits the life and myths of one of the twentieth century's most influential thinkers, correcting the caricature from within.

This episode reconstructs a fictional interview with Sartre, in which he addresses the legends that have grown around his name: the radical existentialist, the man of "bad faith," the slogan-monger of "existence precedes essence." He speaks about his major works, from "Nausea" to "Being and Nothingness," about "Existentialism Is a Humanism" and the lecture that changed how millions understand freedom, and about "The Dirty Hands" and the moral ambiguities of political violence. He reflects on his relationship with Simone de Beauvoir and her authorship of "The Second Sex," on founding "Les Temps modernes," on Indochina and the painful contradictions of his own family history, and on May 1968, when the young people of France looked to him as a symbol of committed intellectual life.
