Winfried Schumann and the Resonances of the Earth

A German physicist does a division: the circumference of the Earth divided by the speed of light. The result — 7.83 hertz — has become the planet's alleged "heartbeat".
In 1952, Winfried Otto Schumann, a German electrical engineer, published with his student the demonstration that the Earth and the ionosphere form a resonant cavity. He will never hear his own voice — but his equations are today the instrument that science uses to count the thunderstorms of the world, to track tropical temperature, and to hunt for hydrocarbon deposits off the coasts.
This episode tells the story of the man, of the cavity, of the measurements that finally came — and it sorts out physics from the promises of healing: Schumann resonances are a measuring instrument, not a medicine.
