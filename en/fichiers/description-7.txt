Beethoven Facing the Silence

Vienna, winter 1826. The greatest composer in the world no longer makes out a single note. In his apartment, questions are written to him in a notebook — and he answers out loud, rough and free.
The most famous deaf man who ever lived, brought back to life through an interview he could never actually hear.

How did a man who went completely deaf compose the Ninth Symphony, the Ode to Joy, the last quartets? Through this fictional interview built on real facts, Beethoven tells of the whistling that began at 26, the Heiligenstadt Letter in which he admitted he wanted to die, the piano with its legs removed and the baton held between his teeth so he could feel the vibrations, that evening of 1824 when a singer had to turn him towards the standing audience he could not hear — and of silence becoming, against all expectation, his greatest master.
