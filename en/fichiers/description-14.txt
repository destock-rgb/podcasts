Simone de Beauvoir — The Philosopher of the Second Sex

A fictional radio portrait of the existentialist philosopher, writer, and feminist activist who wrote The Second Sex and changed the conversation about gender forever.

From a bourgeois Catholic childhood in Paris to the agrégation de philosophie in 1929, from the lifelong partnership with Jean-Paul Sartre to the publication of one of the most controversial books of the twentieth century — this episode traces the arc of a woman who refused to be defined by anyone else's terms.

Simone de Beauvoir was not just a philosopher. She was a novelist, a political activist, an editor of Les Temps Modernes, and a voice for the feminist movement of the 1970s. Her work on the social construction of gender, her analysis of women's oppression across history, and her insistence that "one is not born, but rather becomes, a woman" remain foundational to feminist thought today. This is a fictional reconstruction — not a real interview — brought to you as part of the "Who's That Guy?" portrait series.
