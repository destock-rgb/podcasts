Marie Curie — The Woman With Two Nobel Prizes

What if a discovery could light up the world twice — and cost the woman who made it her life? Born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw in 1867, Marie Curie became the only person ever to receive two Nobel Prizes in two different sciences: physics in 1903, chemistry in 1911.

In this imagined interview, we follow her road: the Polish girl who studied in secret in a "flying university" before boarding the train to the Sorbonne; the meeting with Pierre Curie; the announcement of polonium in July 1898 and radium in December; the terrible years in a dilapidated shed where tons of pitchblende were processed without protection; the first chair ever held by a woman professor at the Sorbonne; the "little Curies" of the First World War; and her daughter Irène, who won a Nobel Prize of her own in 1935. Marie Curie speaks here of her philosophy of work, transmission and perseverance — and of the terrible price paid for a discovery whose danger was still unknown.
