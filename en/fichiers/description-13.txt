Pythagoras — The Music of the Spheres

A philosopher who heard the universe in numbers — and a legend that grew louder than the man himself.

Step into the imagined world of Pythagoras, the Ionian Greek who united mathematics, music, and the heavens into a single vision of cosmic order. This episode of "Who's That Guy?" presents a fictional interview that separates the attested teachings of the historical Pythagoras — metempsychosis, the tetraktys, the mathematical foundations of harmony — from the myths that later centuries have draped over his name. From the monochord to the music of the spheres, from the school of Croton to the burning of its meeting houses, this is a portrait built from the fragments Aristoxenus and Diogenes Laertius left behind.

The Pythagorean school was not the work of one man but of a community of initiates who pursued knowledge as a path to purification. Their ideas — the sphericity of the Earth, the doctrine of proportions, the link between music and ethics — traveled across centuries and shaped the astronomy of Hipparchus, the cosmology of Copernicus, and the physics of Newton. Yet every attribution must be weighed, every legend questioned. This is not a hagiography. It is a conversation with an echo, asking what we can truly know about the man who said that all is number.
